What it does

One place for the things families forget

Drop in a bill or a statement. Mitra Home reads it, files it and reminds you before anything is due.

Today

Open it, and you know what needs you.

A single card for the day: what is overdue, what is on the calendar and the money ahead. Tap Done or Paid right there.

  • Overdue papers, bills and appointments first
  • One person at a time, or the whole family
  • A short brief each morning, a family digest each week
This month

See where the money went, without a spreadsheet.

Drop in your bank and card statements. Each month becomes a card you swipe through: income, spending, what you saved, and every category with every charge behind it.

  • Spending by category against last month
  • Budget lines, subscriptions and price rises
  • Common bank PDFs and CSV exports read with no AI
Calendar

Everyone's dates, in one calendar.

Bills, appointments, classes, school closures, birthdays, renewals and holidays, all from your records. A glance card says how busy the week is and what not to miss.

  • Week, month and year views
  • Weekly classes and school calendars
  • Optional copy to your phone's calendar
Family

Each person, their own card.

Passports that are about to expire, the dentist, the band fundraiser form, the dog's heartworm pill. Children get their own simple page with their items and the family lists, and never see money.

  • Every adult has their own PIN
  • Shared shopping, to-do and packing lists
  • Papers filed by year and type, searchable
Inbox

Drop the paper in. Watch it get filed.

Drag bills, statements, receipts or a photo anywhere onto the Inbox, or paste a screenshot. Each file shows its step while it is read and sorted, and the few that need you wait in plain groups: money, renewals, family, calendar.

  • Drag and drop, paste, or take a photo on the phone
  • Live progress: reading, sorting, filed
  • Exact copies are set aside, never filed twice
Wallet

Every card, and where its number is.

IDs, the car with its plate, insurance, library and store cards, even the Wi-Fi name and trash day, as cards on the PC and the phone. Tap one to see where the number is printed on the real card. The parking meter asks for the plate? It is right there.

  • Car, People, Home and Memberships
  • Expiring within 60 days shows the days left
  • Reference copies: last four digits for IDs and policies
Records, Finance, Settings

Everything kept, and what needs a look first.

Records, Finance and Settings each open on a short summary: one card per area with a number that matters, and a "Needs a look" list (an expired passport, a car inspection, a bill past due, a missing backup). One search finds any record or filed paper.

  • People, Home, Vehicles, Papers and IDs
  • Pay, Keep and Look back for the money
  • Compact lists on the phone, full tables on the PC
How it works

Three steps, no technical knowledge needed

Think of Mitra Home as a tidy family assistant who lives on the computer in your home.

1

You drop papers in

  • Drag a PDF into Add documents
  • Scan a paper with the phone app
  • Or type: "Sam's dentist is Oct 21"
2

Your PC sorts it out

  • Works out what each paper is
  • Pulls out due dates and amounts
  • Files it away, safely, at home
3

You get reminded

  • Today page: what needs you
  • Daily brief and weekly digest
  • Phone reminder and calendar

What is "the AI" doing?

Reading a messy bill is hard for a computer. Mitra Home can ask an AI helper of your choice (for example a Claude, ChatGPT or Gemini plan you already have, or a free model on your own PC) to read it. Before anything is sent, names and account numbers are hidden.

No AI? Still useful.

AI is optional. Common bank statement PDFs, bank CSV exports and Verizon phone bills are read with no AI at all, and reminders, lists, calendar and backups all work without it.

Who can see what

Each adult gets their own PIN. Children and guests get their own simple page with their own items and family lists, and never see money.

Simple or Advanced

Start simple. Turn on more when you are ready.

Most families only need the Simple set. Advanced shows the extra tools for people who enjoy tinkering. Both are free.

Simple, the default

For everyone in the family

  • Five-minute setup chat: family, people, home, PINs
  • Today: what is due and what needs you
  • Bills and due dates, mark as paid
  • Add documents: drop papers, they get filed
  • Family calendar, birthdays, appointments
  • Shared lists and notes
  • Daily brief and weekly family digest
  • Phone app on your home Wi-Fi
  • Automatic weekly backup
Advanced, one switch in Settings

For the family's "tech person"

  • Bank statements, budgets, spending trends
  • Insurance policies and renewal reviews
  • Taxes, home office and year-end papers
  • Work hours and invoices for consultants
  • Choose and switch AI providers
  • Google Calendar, Gmail and Drive links
  • Telegram and Discord family bots
  • Encrypted backups to a second drive
  • Region packs, category rules, optional bank sync

The Simple / Advanced switch is in Settings > Household profile > How much to show. New installs start in Simple; installs that were already running keep seeing everything (Advanced). It only changes what is shown: nothing is deleted or switched off, and both views are free.

Is it really free?

Yes. No subscription, no account. If you use an AI helper, you pay that provider directly under your own plan or key (often a few cents to a dollar a month for a family), or use a free local model.

What do I need?

A Windows PC or Mac that is usually switched on, about 15 minutes, and optionally an Android phone or iPhone. See Install.

Open source

Released under the GNU AGPL. The code will be public once testing with our first families is complete, so anyone can check what it does with their data.
