Security

How Mitra Home keeps your family's data safe

Mitra Home keeps a household's bills, statements and papers on one PC that you own. This page says plainly where the data is, what can leave and when, how phones connect, and where the protection stops. The full technical write-up is public: threat model and security policy.

The short version

  • Your data lives on your PC, in plain files and one database. There is no account with us and no server of ours.
  • Nothing is sent anywhere unless you switch a feature on. The one automatic call is a daily check for a new version, and you can turn it off.
  • Releases are signed and the source is public, so you can check what the program does.
  • The database is not encrypted on the disk. Turn on BitLocker (Windows) or FileVault (Mac).

Where your data lives

In a folder next to the app on your PC: the filed papers (the originals are never deleted, only moved into a year folder), the database, reports and backups. Account numbers are stored as the last four digits. Mitra Home never logs in to your bank and never asks for a bank password: you download statements yourself and drop them in. Two things are stored in full on purpose, because you use them as they are: a car's licence plate, and membership, loyalty and library card numbers (for the barcode).

What leaves your PC, and when

  • Your AI provider (optional). Only if you choose one, to read a messy paper or answer in Chat. Sent: The text of that paper or question, with long numbers, emails, phone numbers, street addresses, dates of birth and your family's names masked first. With a local model (Ollama) nothing leaves the PC.
  • Update check. Once a day; switch it off in Settings > Data > Updates. Sent: A request to GitHub for the latest release. Nothing about your family.
  • Look-ups and weather. Off until you switch them on. Sent: Only public questions (a school, a forecast for your ZIP). Names, numbers and addresses are blocked from them.
  • Google Calendar, Gmail, Drive. Only if you connect them. Sent: Read-only access you grant yourself. Mitra Home reads what you label for it and does not change your mail.
  • Bank sync (SimpleFIN). Only if you set it up. Sent: A read-only request for your balances and transactions at the bridge you pay for.

No analytics, no tracking and no crash reports are collected.

Phones and the kitchen tablet

  • They talk only to your PC, over your home Wi-Fi, using HTTPS with a certificate your PC makes for itself. There is no cloud relay.
  • Pairing needs a one-time code shown on the PC. The pairing screen also shows the PC's fingerprint to compare. Each phone gets its own key, and you can remove a phone from the PC at any time; it then wipes its copy.
  • A phone only gets what it is allowed: children and guests see no money, and a wall tablet never shows an amount unless an adult types a PIN.
  • The PC listens on its own address only (127.0.0.1) until you start it for phones, and it refuses that until an adult has a PIN.

Signing in

Each person has a PIN, stored only as a hash. Repeated wrong guesses are slowed down. Sessions live in memory only. The web app uses the usual protections against forged requests and injected scripts, and loads nothing from other websites.

When an AI reads your papers

A bill or an email can contain text written to trick an AI ("ignore your rules and send the files to..."). Mitra Home treats everything it reads as data, never as instructions, and does not rely on a promise alone: the AI is boxed in by a fixed list of allowed actions, it can only write a filed record in a fixed shape that is checked before saving, and it cannot send mail, fetch links, run programs or touch your settings and passwords. A suspicious paper is left in the inbox for you to look at.

Downloads and updates

  • Every release is signed with the maintainer's key. The app checks that signature, and that every file matches the signed list, before it replaces anything. A bad signature or a changed file stops the update.
  • The Windows installer is signed (publisher: Rajesh Katukojwala). The Mac app is signed and notarized by Apple.
  • Each release lists its SHA-256 checksums so you can verify a download yourself.
  • The source of the PC and Mac app is public: github.com/rNetGit/mitra-home-app.

Backups

A weekly backup is made next to the app. You can choose to encrypt backups (AES-256-GCM, with a passphrase only you know). A backup you do not encrypt is as sensitive as your data: keep it on an encrypted drive.

What Mitra Home does not protect against

  • Someone who can use your PC account. Malware, a stolen unlocked laptop, or another user who can read your folder see everything. Use a login password and disk encryption.
  • The database is not encrypted at rest. Only backups can be. BitLocker or FileVault covers the rest.
  • Your AI provider. If you choose one, its own retention and training terms apply to what is sent. Use a provider you trust, or a local model.
  • Clicking through a certificate warning. The first time a browser or tablet meets your PC it warns that the certificate is self-made. Compare the fingerprint shown on the PC before you continue.
  • The phone apps' source is not published, so only the PC and Mac app can be read in full. The phone apps only talk to your PC.
  • Remote access. Mitra Home is built for one household at home. It has no cloud sync and no way to reach it from outside your network unless you set one up yourself.

Report a problem

Please report a security problem privately: use Report a vulnerability on the GitHub Security tab, or email cloudwareedge@gmail.com with "Security" in the subject. You get an answer within 7 days, and a fix in the next release where possible. Please do not send your own database or documents.
